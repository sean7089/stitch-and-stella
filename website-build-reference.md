# Local service website reference

A practical reference for building a small-business website: what belongs in the first build, what belongs in ongoing care, and what the public case studies say actually moves calls and bookings.

Source studied: [Bigfoot Build Studio](https://www.bigfootbuildstudio.com) (public pages, service pages, pricing, capabilities matrix, and the studio’s own `llms.txt` / `llms-full.txt` indexes), reviewed November 1, 2026. This file restates the structure and lessons in plain language. It is not a copy of that site, its design, its client websites, or its marketing copy. Published prices below are that studio’s prices, kept here only so you can see how a real offer is packaged.

---

## What this kind of studio is selling

The offer is not “a pretty website.” It is a phone-ringing system for local service businesses, sold in three layers:

1. **A one-time custom build** that replaces a slow template site.
2. **A month-to-month plan** that hosts it, protects it, and (on higher plans) grows local search.
3. **Optional marketing engines** for social posts and Google Ads.

Positioning that shows up everywhere on the site:

- Hand-built, mobile-first, very fast. They contrast this with WordPress, Wix, Squarespace, and plugin-heavy agency sites.
- Founder does the work. No junior account manager, no long contract.
- The client owns the domain, the site, the brand files, and the ad accounts.
- Pricing is published. The pitch is that traditional agencies hide a $5,000+ quote and lock people into 12-month retainers.
- The site’s job is a call, a text, or a booking, not a brochure.

Who they say they build for: counseling and medical clinics, chiropractors, home-service trades (HVAC, roofing, flooring, electrical), auto shops, luxury vehicle rental, and food or catering brands. The pattern is the same across those industries. Trust, speed, and “near me” search decide who gets the job.

Typical timeline they publish:

- Custom site live in **2 to 3 weeks**.
- Ads or social can produce inquiries in the **first week** after launch.
- Maps and local search movement in **30 to 60 days**, with compounding leads over **90 days**.
- They claim they will say no up front if they do not believe the business will get a return.

A first sales conversation, as they describe it: 15 minutes by phone, text, or video. Look at the current site and local Google visibility. No slide deck.

## The base build

**$850 one-time**, described as the starting price. This is the website itself, before any monthly plan.

What they say is in it:

- Custom design around that business, not a shared template.
- Mobile-first layout, including a dark/light visual system.
- Speed target they advertise as under half a second on a phone, with a written **100/100** on Google’s Core Web Vitals (Lighthouse performance, accessibility, best practices, and SEO).
- Click-to-call and lead forms that land in email and on the phone.
- Logos and photos prepared so they stay sharp on phones and large screens.
- A starter Google Search and Google Business Profile setup: the business is identifiable to Google, not just pretty.
- Baseline structured data (Schema.org JSON-LD) so search engines can read the business name, type, services, and location.
- Encrypted routing of form leads.

What a finished base site needs, translated into a build checklist:

| Piece | Why it exists |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage | Who you are, where you work, what to do next. One obvious action: call, text, or book. |
| Service pages | One page per real service, written for how a customer searches, not for how the owner talks internally. |
| Proof | Reviews, photos of real work, names of people, prices or starting prices when the business will publish them. |
| Contact | Phone, address or service area, hours, email, and a short form. |
| Click-to-call | Sticky or repeated on mobile. Most of these customers are on a phone. |
| Images | Compressed modern formats (they use AVIF/WebP). A huge photo is a slow page. |
| Identity for Google | Business name, category, address or service area, hours, and matching info on the website and the Google Business Profile. |
| Redirects | If you replace an old site, keep the old URLs or redirect them so existing Google history is not thrown away. |
| Ownership | Domain, hosting login, analytics, and Google Business Profile stay in the client’s name. |

### Google Ads

**$850 / month** management fee. Covers Google Search and Local Services Ads up to about **$15,000 / month** of spend. The budget itself goes straight to Google.

What they say is inside the management:

- Ads land on a fast page for that service and neighborhood, not the generic homepage.
- A large block list of junk searches: “free,” “DIY,” “how to,” “jobs,” and similar. They say junk terms can eat 40–60% of a budget.

Why speed is part of an ads offer: Google’s Quality Score rewards a landing page that loads fast and matches the search. A higher score means a lower cost per click.

## What actually goes into the build

This is the ingredient list behind the plans. Use it as a spec when you build your own sites.

### 1. Speed is the product

Their published performance bar:

- Time to first byte under about 50 ms.
- Largest contentful paint under 0.8 seconds.
- Interaction delay under 40 ms.
- Layout shift at zero.
- Lighthouse 100 across performance, accessibility, best practices, and SEO.

How they get there, in their own stack description:

- **Astro** for mostly static pages, with **React** only on the interactive pieces (an “islands” approach, so the whole page is not a heavy app).
- Code for widgets loaded only when needed.
- Hosted on **Cloudflare** (Pages and Workers), so the file is served from a location near the visitor.
- Images converted to AVIF or WebP.
- No database query on every page view. Pages are compiled ahead of time.
- No plugin pile. Forms, galleries, and SEO are part of the site, not third-party add-ons.

The business reason they repeat: Google has said a large share of mobile visitors leave if a page takes longer than about 3 seconds. A 4–5 second clinic site was the before-state in their healthcare case study. Speed is framed as lost calls, not as a trophy score.

For your own builds, the transferable rule is: ship HTML that is already ready, compress images, and do not load a page builder, a dozen plugins, or a full app framework for a brochure.

### 2. The page is built around the buying action

Static “Contact us” forms are treated as the weak version. The stronger version is a small tool that answers a question and collects a better lead:

| Business | Tool they describe |
| --- | --- |
| Clinic or counseling | A few questions that match the visitor to the right clinician or specialty. |
| Contractor | An estimator from size, scope, or symptom of the job. |
| Auto shop | A diagnostic intake: vehicle, then concern, then a straight assessment and a way to book. |
| Fleet rental | A savings or booking calculator that keeps the reservation in-house instead of on a marketplace that takes 25–30%. |
| Food and catering | A flavor explorer, a tin or menu builder, or a party estimator. |

On a live auto-shop example in their portfolio, that intake is a 3-step flow: pick the vehicle platform, pick the concern, then send details. The same page also has a flat-rate inspection price, service list, shop story, hours, phone, and a form. That is the pattern: proof, price, tool, and a way to reach a person.

### 3. Local pages are how they sell “show up in the next town over”

A single homepage rarely ranks for every suburb. Their growth offer is dedicated pages for the places customers actually live.

- Growth: 3 nearby cities or suburbs.
- Top plan: on the order of 25, sometimes described as 25–50+.
- Add-on: 5 more for $250 a month.

Each page is meant to name the neighborhood, landmarks, and the service someone there would search for, and to match the Google Business Profile’s service area.

Do this carefully. A pile of near-duplicate city pages with the town name swapped is a doorway-page pattern, and Google has penalized that for years. The useful version is a page that is true for that place: real service area, real drive-time context, real photos or jobs, and unique answers. Three strong suburb pages beat fifty thin ones.

Their own studio site demonstrates the aggressive version: dozens of city URLs, each with landmarks, a ZIP, coordinates, and a list of “web design + city” phrases. That is their marketing site selling web design in many cities. It is a useful look at the tactic. It is a poor template to copy blindly onto a client site.

### 4. Google Maps is a separate job from the website

They say more than 70% of local phone calls go to the top few map results. The website and the Google Business Profile have to agree:

- Same name, phone, address or service area, hours, and categories.
- Fresh photos and posts. This is why the social add-on includes the Business Profile, not only Instagram.
- Reviews, and a simple way to ask for them.
- Service and location pages that match what the profile claims.

Tracking they mention: a geo-grid (they name Local Falcon) that checks map rank from several points around the city, not one vanity keyword.

### 5. Structured data and files for AI search

“Generative engine optimization,” in their plain-language FAQ, means: when someone asks ChatGPT or Google’s AI overview “who is the best X near me,” the assistant can verify your services, area, hours, and reputation and name you.

Ingredients they list:

- Schema.org JSON-LD for the right type of business (`MedicalClinic`, `AutoRepair`, `VeterinaryCare`, `Bakery`, `AutoRental`, `OfferCatalog`, and so on), plus pages for individual people when the people are the product (clinicians).
- Stable IDs so the same business is one entity across pages, not a new blob of text each time.
- Links to public references (Wikipedia or Wikidata) for the city and the business type, so the graph is grounded.
- `llms.txt`: a short index of what the business is, what it sells, and where the important URLs are.
- `llms-full.txt`: the long version, with services, areas, FAQs, and proof.
- A normal XML sitemap, and fast pages so crawlers can fetch them.
- `robots.txt` that allows major search and AI crawlers, and blocks private API routes.

They still say classic Google Search and Maps work is required. The AI layer is the same facts, written so a machine can quote them without guessing.

For your own sites, a useful minimum is: one clear schema block, an FAQ that answers real questions, consistent NAP (name, address, phone), and a short markdown file that states facts a model would need. Do not invent reviews, rankings, or credentials.

### 6. Lead delivery

A form that only stores a row in a database is not the product. Their version:

- Spam filtering.
- Email through a transactional sender (they name Resend).
- SMS alerts (they name Twilio, with the business-texting registration that carriers require).
- An encrypted lead log (they name Cloudflare D1).
- For ads, a click ID stored with the lead so spend can be tied to a call or form.
- A honeypot field on their own contact form (“leave this empty”) plus an SMS consent checkbox.

The client should see the lead on their phone, not in a portal they never open. The portal is for proof: queries, clicks, calls, form fills, ad spend.

### 7. Reporting

Higher plans include a live view of Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4: queries, impressions, clicks, calls, forms. The promise is official Google data, not a PDF of made-up rankings.

### 8. Industry shape, from the case studies and portfolio

**Multi-clinician counseling practice (Cypress / northwest Houston).**  
Before: a legacy agency retainer and a plugin CMS that took about 4.5 seconds on a phone. Patients bounced before they saw a number.  
Built: fast pages, a profile for each of 11 clinicians, an interactive therapist matcher, medical-clinic structured data, and about 15 suburb hubs (Cypress, Bridgeland, Towne Lake, Copperfield, Tomball, Katy, The Woodlands, and related ZIPs). Specialties called out by name: EMDR, neurofeedback, brainspotting, couples counseling.  
Claimed after 60 days: inquiries doubled, 83+ matcher completions, about 39,000 Google impressions and 494 organic clicks, top-three map visibility in northwest Houston ZIPs, and thousands of AI crawler hits. The expensive retainer went away.

**Luxury and exotic car rental (Houston).**  
Before: no local presence, competing with national rental sites.  
Built: fast pages with real fleet photos, about 29 pages for wealthy pockets of the metro (River Oaks, Memorial, Bridgeland, Cinco Ranch, The Woodlands, Sugar Land, West University, and others), rental and offer schema, a long `llms-full.txt`, and a booking path that skips marketplace commission.  
Claimed in under 60 days: number-one organic placements in those pockets, about 11,700 Google impressions, 2,800+ AI citations, and 226+ direct booking starts.

**Gourmet retail (Houston).**  
Flavor explorer, event-tin configurator, bakery and offer schema. They cite 2,100+ AI citations.

**Mobile catering.**  
About 20 neighborhood delivery hubs, a party estimator, and structured data for more than one business entity on the same brand.

**German auto specialist (Spring Branch).**  
Diagnostic estimator, a “versus the dealer” savings calculator, and repair-shop pages for Spring Branch, Memorial, and the Energy Corridor.  
The live site shows the same anatomy in public: specialty and price in the headline ($150 inspection), a step-by-step intake, individual service blocks, a founder story, video proof of real work, hours, address, click-to-call, and a booking form.

**Veterinary and equine chiropractic (Lubbock).**  
Pet mobility questionnaire, veterinary-care schema, and a handful of West Texas corridor pages (they say six).

Lesson across all six: the website is a set of specific pages plus one interactive qualifier, not a five-page template with the logo swapped.

## A build you can reuse

Use this as the generic spec. Fill in the business. Ignore their brand names.

### Discovery (the 15-minute version, then a deeper pass)

- What does a good lead look like, and what should be turned away?
- What are the 3 to 7 services that make money?
- Which 3 suburbs are worth a page, and which are vanity?
- What proof exists: reviews, photos, licenses, prices, staff names?
- What does the current site already rank for? Keep those URLs.
- Who owns the domain, hosting, Google profile, and analytics today?

### Base site

- Custom layout, mobile first, one primary action.
- Homepage, core service pages, about, contact.
- Real photos, compressed.
- Phone, hours, service area, and a form that texts and emails the owner.
- Schema for the business type.
- Analytics and Search Console on the client’s Google account.
- Speed checked on a phone, not only on desktop.
- Old URLs redirected.

### Care, after launch

- Hosting, SSL, backups or a way to redeploy.
- Small copy and photo edits.
- Watch the speed score when new images are added.
- Spam control on forms.

### Growth, when they want more calls

- One interactive tool matched to how customers decide.
- Three suburb or neighborhood pages with real local detail.
- Google Business Profile aligned with the site, plus a review ask.
- A simple dashboard: queries, clicks, calls, forms.

### Wider area and AI answers, only if the business truly serves that area

- More location pages, each with something true to say.
- FAQ answers written the way customers ask.
- A short machine-readable facts file.
- Monthly review of which queries moved and which competitors passed them.

### If they also want ads or social

- Ads only to the matching service page, with junk terms blocked and a budget cap.
- Social tied to services and to the Google profile, with approval before posting.

## Operating details worth copying as habits

- Put the price on the page. Hidden pricing was the thing they attack in competitors.
- Say what is not included. Hosting is monthly. Ads budget is separate. Extra pages and tools are line items.
- Month-to-month, and the client keeps the accounts.
- One person answers the phone. Their published contact is a direct founder line and email, with weekday, Saturday, and weekend support hours listed on the service pages.
- A free speed and SEO check is the top-of-site lead magnet: type a URL, optional email for the longer report.
- The contact form asks for name, email, phone, and project details, with explicit text-message consent.

## What not to lift

- Their name, visual system, slogans, or plan names.
- Client site designs, photos, or page copy.
- Invented performance numbers. The figures in the case studies are their claims about their clients. Treat them as examples of what to measure (load time, inquiries, impressions, bookings), not as guarantees you can repeat.
- A city-page factory. Location pages only where the business really works, and only with content that is not a find-and-replace of the city name.
